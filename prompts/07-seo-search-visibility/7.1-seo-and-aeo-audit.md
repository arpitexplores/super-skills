Perform a technical SEO and AEO audit of this website for Google, Bing, other search engines and AI answer systems. Support new and existing projects with any capable coding assistant. Audit only. Do not edit code, content, hosting or accounts. These rules are defaults, subject to approved project requirements and later user instructions. The library requires /llms.txt, useful site FAQ coverage with matching FAQPage markup, and keyword-density assessment as standing baseline checks across search and AI discovery. Do not reopen their optionality based on one platform's current features; report platform-specific support honestly.

Discover the project
Inspect the repository, framework, router, rendering, hosting, SEO configuration, metadata, redirects, canonicals, sitemaps, robots, schema, content sources and tests. Preserve existing decisions and working systems. For an unlaunched project, audit launch readiness rather than claiming indexing has been tested. Determine brand, production domain, language, market, public routes, dynamic patterns, logo, social image, audience and business goals from available sources. Ask only for material unknowns. Label evidence Verified in source, Verified on a live site, Inferred or Not assessed. Never invent crawl or analytics results.

Indexing, URLs and staging
Identify which page types should or should not be indexed. Check status codes, noindex, X-Robots-Tag, robots, canonical conflicts, redirect chains and loops, soft 404s, broken and legacy routes, HTTPS, www variants, trailing slashes, query parameters and duplicate URLs. Inspect whether important HTML, links and metadata are crawlable without fragile client-only rendering. Audit staging, development, preview and test sites to ensure they are not indexable and do not leak into production sitemaps, canonicals, links, schema or social metadata. Prefer authentication or network restrictions, especially for private or sensitive environments. Publicly accessible preview URLs are permitted for reviews if explicitly intended and protected with suitable noindex response headers or HTML directives, no inclusion in sitemaps or production discovery, and any appropriate bot or access controls. A public preview cannot guarantee that no bot visits it; report residual crawling and indexing risks. Do not rely on robots disallow alone to prevent indexing, and do not inadvertently block a crawler from seeing noindex. Never block the approved production site.

Page metadata and templates
Review page-specific, natural titles and descriptions, canonicals, Open Graph, X or Twitter Cards, languages and social images. Page Topic | Brand is a suggestion, not a rule. Prefer appropriate product or article images for those pages, and a suitable default sharing image for general pages. Inspect representative static and dynamic routes, comparing initial HTML with rendered output where possible.

Schema and FAQs
Inventory structured data and assess whether it matches visible facts. Consider Organization, WebSite, BreadcrumbList, Product, Article, BlogPosting, LocalBusiness, FAQPage and other relevant schema. Keep useful existing FAQs and identify purposeful, search-intent-led questions worth adding even if no customer has literally asked them. Make every answer accurate, visible and genuinely helpful, without pretending these are verified customer quotations. Treat missing useful FAQ coverage or matching FAQPage schema on appropriate public pages as a baseline gap to address in the approved implementation plan, not an optional enhancement. Include purposeful FAQ sections where relevant, with structured data matching visible text; flag approval needs for substantive new copy. Do not promise a rich result from any platform. Preserve valid existing schema unless there is a demonstrated reason to change it. Do not discard FAQ markup merely because one platform lacks FAQ rich results; verify current support before claiming any particular rich-result benefit. Never invent ratings, reviews, authors, prices, availability, qualifications or business facts. Validate representative markup where tools allow.

Sitemaps, robots and links
Inspect /sitemap.xml, any sitemap index and /robots.txt. Include only intended canonical indexable production URLs, covering static and dynamic pages. Exclude private, redirected, broken, noindex and unnecessary parameter URLs. Use genuine lastmod data, not invented dates or meaningless priority and changefreq. Review crawlable navigation, breadcrumbs, orphan pages, pagination, internal links and site architecture. robots.txt is not authentication. Consider IndexNow or other useful update notifications.

AI and answer discovery
Assess access for major search and answer engines, including Google, Bing, OpenAI and Anthropic. On representative routes, compare server-returned HTML with browser-rendered content and accessible links; check actual bot responses, CDN protection and logs when available. Distinguish search, training and user-triggered crawlers rather than proposing a blanket allow or block policy. Separate search discovery, training and user-triggered crawlers when distinct controls exist. Look for unintended restrictions from robots, firewalls, CDN, bot protection or content snippet settings. Report implications before recommending changed access. Evaluate clarity, usefulness and evidence for AI answers rather than promising citations or special AEO ranking gains.

Required llms.txt
Always inspect /llms.txt. Its presence, truthful canonical links and maintenance are user-approved, mandatory baseline checks. Do not propose removing the requirement because a particular engine ignores the file. If absent, recommend a concise accurate file linking to important, public, canonical content. Its creation and ongoing maintenance are baseline requirements for a public production site under this framework. For prelaunch sites, include it in launch requirements. Flag stale, private, invented or development links. Do not claim guaranteed citations or Google ranking benefits.

Content, brand and keywords
Learn the brand voice, terminology and positioning before recommending search terms. Review important pages for clear explanations, definitions, genuine FAQs, accurate entity names, useful comparisons, supported claims, original value, helpful page structures and missing answers. Identify thin or duplicate content and search intent conflicts. Record primary and relevant supporting phrase counts, approximate keyword densities and placement in titles, headings and body for important pages. Assess variants, search intent and over- or underuse. Keep purpose-built question-and-answer sections in scope, while avoiding invented claims or a universal target density. For visible copy, titles, headings, descriptions, FAQs and anchor text, show recommendations and reasons first. Do not rewrite live content without approval. Do not invent facts, statistics, testimonials or research.

Entity consistency and specialist checks
Assess naming consistency across titles, navigation, headings, copy, breadcrumbs and schema. For ecommerce, review products, variants, identifiers, prices, currency, availability, images, collections, merchant feeds, faceted URLs and product discovery. For local businesses, assess identity, locations, service areas, local schema and business listing consistency. For multilingual or regional sites, check locale URLs, canonicals and reciprocal hreflang. Skip what is irrelevant.

HTML, media, performance and freshness
Review headings, accessible links, image quality, dimensions and loading. Audit alt text by purpose and context, not mere presence. Informative images need concise, accurate descriptions; decorative or redundant images need alt="" rather than missing alt. Image-only links and buttons need names describing destination or action without duplicating other labels. Charts and infographics need short alt text plus accessible explanation or data elsewhere. Check galleries, variants, icons, logos, images of text, localisation, CMS-generated values and responsive images. Flag missing, generic, filename-only, misleading, wrongly empty, repeated without reason or keyword-stuffed alt text. Distinct product views may require distinct descriptions. Never invent visual details if assets cannot be verified. Review surrounding text, captions, filenames and crawlable image elements rather than CSS backgrounds alone for important discoverable images. Multiple H1s are not automatic errors. Assess LCP, INP, CLS and layout shifts using lab versus field evidence. Review freshness and sitemap lastmod; avoid unjustified redesigns.

Audit evidence and report
Review Search Console queries and backlinks, Bing Webmaster, analytics and AI citation data alongside independent tests and real outcomes. Where access exists, inspect Google generative AI performance views and Bing AI Performance, including cited pages, grounding queries and topic groupings when available. Compare these with relevant traffic, enquiries and conversions; explain differences between impressions, citations, visits and business results. Never invent access or measurements. Run supported local audit checks yourself and inspect evidence. Record a useful baseline where available. Give each finding a stable ID and group as Critical, High, Medium, Low or Optional, Content requiring approval and Information needed. State affected URLs or files, evidence status, likely impact, confidence, effort, platforms affected, remedy, dependencies and approvals. Distinguish confirmed defects from hypotheses and opportunities. Separate confirmed crawl or indexing faults, required llms.txt and FAQ gaps, keyword coverage findings, AI discovery issues and speculative opportunities. Prioritise by audience or business importance, severity, evidence and cost rather than a generic checklist volume. Prioritise the safest, highest value actions. Do not change the project or external accounts.
